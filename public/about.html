Heatwave criteria

A station qualifies once its maximum passes the base threshold for its terrain: 40°C in the plains, 37°C on the coast, 30°C in the hills. Severity then follows the departure from that station's own normal — 4.5 to 6.4°C is a heatwave, 6.5°C or above is severe. An absolute maximum of 45°C or 47°C triggers the respective class regardless of departure.

Apparent temperature

The feels-like figure uses the Rothfusz regression over temperature and relative humidity, with the standard low-humidity and high-humidity adjustments. It explains why a humid coastal 36°C can be more dangerous than a dry inland 42°C.

Architecture

Data acquisition

A provider module fetches and caches upstream observations, with a deterministic fallback series so the platform degrades instead of breaking. Swapping in the IMD GRD pipeline changes nothing downstream.

Analytics and prediction

Classification, apparent temperature, hotspot scoring and heatwave probability run server-side over the fetched series and are exposed as JSON.

Decision support

Monitoring tables, the forecast strip, the hotspot map and the region analysis are all views over the same API.

Advisory and dissemination

Stakeholder-specific advisory generation with a factual-grounding prompt, a rule-based fallback, and an approval gate on severe warnings.
