Reading the classification

IMD does not declare a heatwave on a single number. A station qualifies once its maximum passes a base threshold — 40°C in the plains, 37°C on the coast, 30°C in the hills — and then the departure from that station's own normal decides severity.

Normal Heat watch, departure 3.0 to 4.4°C Heatwave, departure 4.5 to 6.4°C Severe, departure 6.5°C or more

An absolute maximum of 45°C triggers a heatwave and 47°C a severe heatwave, whatever the departure.

Why heatwave monitoring matters

Heatwaves are among the deadliest weather hazards in India. Long spells of extreme heat raise the risk of heat stroke, dehydration and crop loss, and hot nights stop the body from recovering. Outdoor workers, farmers, older people and young children are affected first.

Tapmaan tracks maximum temperature, night minimum and heat index at twenty-five stations, and compares each reading with that station's own normal. This is how the India Meteorological Department declares a heatwave, so the same reading of 41°C can mean a warning in one city and an ordinary summer day in another.

Use the seven-day heatwave forecast to see what is coming, check the heatwave hotspots for regions under the most stress, and subscribe to heatwave alerts for your city. Official warnings are issued by IMD, and the national heat action guidelines are published by NDMA.

Tapmaan is a student project built for use case KJS-CES-01, Climate Intelligence for Heatwave Monitoring, Prediction and Early Warning. Classification follows India Meteorological Department heatwave criteria. This is not an official warning service — for operational decisions, follow IMD.
