Hotspot identification

A hotspot is not the hottest place. The score combines departure from local normal, how long the spell persists, and whether night temperatures fall far enough for the body to recover — which is what drives heat mortality.

Every monitored station, coloured by current classification.

How the score is built

Four components, capped at 100. Departure above the local normal carries the most weight, then persistence of the spell, then the night-time minimum above 26°C, then the humidity contribution to apparent temperature.

Night-time minima matter disproportionately. A city that cools to 22°C after a 44°C day is far less dangerous than one that stays at 30°C after 41°C, because the second gives no overnight recovery.
